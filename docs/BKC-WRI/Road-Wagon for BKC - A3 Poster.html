THE PROBLEM

BKC concentrates ~200,000 daily commuters within 3.7 km² peninsula, with poor connectivity . Three transit anchors — Bandra (Western Line), Kurla (Central / Harbour), and the new BKC Metro on Line 3 — sit just outside. The last 1.5 km to office spaces is driven by car or cab, congesting BKC's internal roads every weekday.

THE PROPOSAL

Deploy patent-pending Road-Wagons (12 m BEV, 1+6 doors, 92 pax) as a BRT, total 14 across two complementary services on existing BKC roads.
Setup: modular 1.1 m raised platforms — installed overnight, No dedicated corridor.

Dwell time ~ 30 sec /stop (same as metro).      Speed ~ 1min / 1km congestion-free.

TRANSFORMATION VISION
Two route options, one fleet — R1 across perimeter loop, R2 spine connecting stations.

ROUTE OPTIONS
MINI-PILOT · entry option
Lowest-risk proof point: Kurla Stn ↔ BKC Telephone Exchange.
  • Length: ~1.2 km · 2 platform stops
  • Fleet: 2 Road-Wagons, 2 platforms
  • > 1,000 vehicles/day off BKC roads
  • Conditional on lane priority on this corridor
Smallest viable footprint to validate boarding-rate & modal-shift assumptions.
R1 — PERIMETER LOOP
Continuous circulator hitting every office cluster on BKC's outer ring.
  • Length: ~4.4 km · 10 platform stops
  • Round-trip cycle: ~9 min
  • Headway: 90 s (6 buses on route)
  • Throughput: ~3,700 pphpd
Best for: intra-BKC circulation & peak-hour office distribution.
R2 — SPINE
Shuttle linking BKC Metro ↔ Bandra ↔ Kurla ↔ E. Express Hwy.
  • ~5.4 km one-way · 10 platform stops
  • One-way 12 min · Round-trip 24 min
  • Headway ~3 min (8 buses) · ~1,840 pphpd
Extendability: double platforms unlock 96 Road-Wagons on R2 — ~22,000 pphpd, matching today's average BKC footfall.
